Aleksandar Vucic conceded defeat on the night of Sunday, 25 October, after Serbia's snap parliamentary election. <mark>"It is clear that we have lost these elections,"</mark> the former president said, according to Al Jazeera, as projections gave the student-led Student List a majority. The result would end 14 years of rule by his Serbian Progressive Party (SNS), which our [pre-election report](/blog/2026-10-25-serbia-votes-in-snap-election-as-students-challenge-vucic/) described as a close race.

## What happened

### Why did Vucic concede Serbia's election?

Vucic spoke at about 9:30 pm local time, citing a stratified sample of votes, according to Balkan Insight. He said the Student List was leading with <mark>135–140 of the 250 seats</mark>, against about 105 for his list, Al Jazeera reports. A government needs 126 seats.

![Key figure card: the Student List is projected to win 135 to 140 of 250 seats](/images/2026-10-27-vucic-concedes-as-serbias-student-list-wins-election/key-card.svg)

The figures are projections, not official seat totals. Vucic himself noted that final results were not yet available, according to Al Jazeera.

### How many votes did the Student List win?

Vote counts differ slightly by source. The watchdog CRTA's preliminary count gave the Student List <mark>51.47%</mark> and Vucic's "Aleksandar Vucic – United Serbia" coalition 40.42%, according to European Western Balkans. The Republic Electoral Commission (RIK) count, based on 97% of polling stations, put them at 52.65% and 39.22%, Bernama reports. Apart from minority lists, no other list crossed the threshold, and invalid ballots were 1.55%, European Western Balkans says.

![Bar chart: Student List 52.65% and United Serbia 39.22% in the RIK count at 97% of polling stations](/images/2026-10-27-vucic-concedes-as-serbias-student-list-wins-election/vote-chart.svg)

### Record turnout in Serbia's snap election

The State Election Commission reported <mark>64.9% turnout by 6 pm</mark>, the highest since Yugoslavia broke up in 1992, Al Jazeera reports. Balkan Insight notes that this compares with 51.93% at the same hour in 2023. About 6.5 million people were eligible to vote, per Al Jazeera.

![Bar chart: turnout at 6 pm was 64.9% in 2026 against 51.93% in 2023](/images/2026-10-27-vucic-concedes-as-serbias-student-list-wins-election/turnout-chart.svg)

### Irregularities and violence reported on election day

CRTA said it saw pressure on voters or election workers at almost a third of the 500 polling stations it monitored, and ballot-secrecy failures, according to Al Jazeera. Balkan Insight reports that CRTA recorded ballot photographing at 80% of its monitored Belgrade stations, a rate it said it had never seen. RIK president Vladimir Dimitrijevic called the incidents mostly minor.

Balkan Insight also reports that prosecutors opened an investigation into a fight involving masked individuals in Pancevo, and that two journalists were attacked in Pavlovac, near Vranje, where two suspects were arrested.

## Background

### Why Serbian students were protesting

In November 2024 a railway station canopy collapsed in Novi Sad, <mark>killing 16 people</mark>. The protests that followed grew into a broader movement against alleged corruption and repression, which Vucic and the SNS deny, according to Al Jazeera. The movement turned into an election list, the Student List, headed by Ilija Srdanovic, per European Western Balkans.

![Timeline from Vucic's concession through final results to the 126 seats needed for a government](/images/2026-10-27-vucic-concedes-as-serbias-student-list-wins-election/timeline.svg)

### How long the SNS had governed

The SNS has governed since 2012. Vucic resigned as president in September to run for prime minister, Al Jazeera reports, and headed his coalition's list.

## Why it matters

For Serbia, a student-led parliamentary majority would be a break with more than a decade of one-party dominance. Student List candidate Radomir Saicic called the result the start of a <mark>"new chapter"</mark>, according to Al Jazeera, and Srdanovic said that "tonight Serbia has won" and that he was proud of those who "stood up to tyranny," European Western Balkans reports.

### What the EU said about Serbia's election

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the EU is ready to support reforms on the rule of law, media freedom and anticorruption, according to Al Jazeera. European Western Balkans carries a headline quoting a Commissioner saying that "the door to the EU is open for Serbia", though the article text was not available to us.

Tens of thousands celebrated in Belgrade, and crowds dismantled a pro-Vucic tent camp in a central park, Al Jazeera reports. Balkan Insight says hooded men were seen entering that camp, with police allowing access, until Vucic conceded.

## What to watch

- **Final results:** According to Bernama, RIK must announce final results within 96 hours of receiving all polling-station protocols, and then distribute parliamentary mandates within 10 days.
- **Seat counts:** Whether the official allocation matches the 135–140 seats projected, and clears the 126 needed.
- **Government formation:** The sources we read do not say who would become prime minister; the students have a leaderless structure, so the choice is an open question.
- **Complaints about the vote:** How CRTA's irregularity findings and the investigations in Pancevo and Pavlovac develop.

## Editor's Insight

The most notable feature of the result is the gap between the two accounts of the vote. Vucic accepted a sample-based projection on election night, before official numbers, which lowers the risk of a disputed outcome. Whether that holds will depend on the final RIK count, and on how the SNS responds once seats are allocated. That is a possibility to watch rather than an established outcome.

The turnout figure suggests the contest mobilised voters far beyond 2023, when turnout at the same hour was nearly 13 percentage points lower, according to Balkan Insight. High turnout is consistent with a movement that began in a disaster and kept organising for nearly two years, though the sources do not say which groups drove the increase.

The harder test comes after the vote. The Student List has no single leader, and the sources do not describe its programme on the EU, Russia or Kosovo. Von der Leyen's offer of support on rule of law and media freedom shows Brussels will engage, but how a new government would balance the EU against Serbia's other partners is unknown. Analysts may read the result alongside other long-ruling parties facing voters, but each country's political system differs.

The contrast with Brazil on the same weekend is striking. There, the losing camp is [seeking to annul Lula's win](/blog/2026-10-27-lula-wins-fourth-term-as-bolsonaro-seeks-annulment/); in Serbia, a long-dominant leader conceded on election night. The Student List and Brussels are the immediate winners, and the SNS the clear loser. Moscow and Beijing face uncertainty about a partner that Vucic had kept close.
