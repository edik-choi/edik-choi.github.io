On 22 October 2026, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) issued what it called a "serious warning": <mark>the conflict "will extend" to US bases in the region</mark> if the US military keeps escorting ships through the Strait of Hormuz. According to AFP, the statement came as Iranian forces said four more ships had been hit overnight, and AP reported that <mark>Lloyd's List Intelligence counted 28 attacks on ships in October</mark>, the highest monthly figure since the war began.

## What happened

### What did the IRGC warn the United States about?

In a statement on its Sepah News website, quoted by AFP, the IRGC said: "The US military must know that if this trend continues, the conflict will extend to its bases in the region." US escorts through the waterway have increased, AFP reported, and Iran has responded by stepping up attacks on ships that try to pass without its authorization.

### How many ships have been hit?

Tehran said on Thursday that seven oil tankers caught fire or were damaged overnight, after being hit by Iran or striking naval mines, according to AP via The Globe and Mail. AP said it could not independently confirm this, and the report did not mention casualties. Separately, Fox News, citing Tasnim News Agency, reported that the IRGC said it struck four ships in the Persian Gulf for conducting ship-to-ship oil transfers.

![Key-figure card showing 28 attacks on ships in October 2026, the most in any month since the war began](/images/2026-10-23-iran-warns-us-bases-as-hormuz-tanker-attacks-hit-october-high/ship-attacks.svg)

The UK Maritime Trade Operations monitor said October had the most reported attacks since March, but did not report the latest incidents, and AP noted that some shippers have become reluctant to report them. The true count may therefore be different from the published figures.

### What did Trump say?

According to Fox News, Donald Trump said in a Truth Social post that Iran is doing "everything they can" to drive up oil prices before the US midterm elections, and claimed Iran is staging fake hits and mines in the strait. Those claims are Trump's and were not independently verified in the reports cited here. Al Jazeera's live coverage of the day also reported that Trump claimed oil shipments through the strait exceed pre-war levels, while Iran threatened US bases.

## Background

### Why is the Strait of Hormuz at the centre of the war?

The war began with US and Israeli attacks on Iran on 28 February, AP reported. Iran claims control of the strait, which AFP says carried about one-fifth of the world's oil supplies before the war and is about 30 km (19 miles) wide at its narrowest point. AP describes the strait as Iran's key point of leverage in any talks with Washington.

### How did the US blockade and the escorts come about?

Washington keeps a naval blockade of Iranian ports to pressure Iran's economy. According to Fox News, US Central Command marked 100 days of the blockade and said it has kept <mark>at least $25 billion of Iranian crude</mark> off the market; strict enforcement resumed on 14 July 2026.

![Timeline from the start of the war on 28 February to the IRGC warning on 22 October and the US midterms on 3 November](/images/2026-10-23-iran-warns-us-bases-as-hormuz-tanker-attacks-hit-october-high/timeline.svg)

Diplomacy has stalled. AP reported that a June deal with conditions similar to Iran's current ones <mark>fell apart within days of both sides signing it</mark>. Iran's latest proposal would end the war and reopen the strait fully within a week if the US meets several conditions, including ending its blockade; Trump rejected it last month, though indirect discussions have continued.

## Why it matters

### How oil is moving through the strait under escort

Per AP, tankers are crossing under US military protection, often at night with location systems off, along a lane near Oman. This "dark shuttle" has returned Gulf oil shipments to roughly pre-war levels, <mark>but at high cost</mark>: spot tanker rates have reached hundreds of thousands of dollars a day. A return to full fighting would likely cause another price spike, AP said.

### Why Saudi Arabia's pipeline matters

Saudi Aramco says its East-West pipeline, which bypasses the strait, has risen from 2 million to <mark>7 million barrels per day</mark>. Fox News, citing the IEA, reported that Saudi output fell from 10 million to 7 million barrels per day in the war's first six months and recovered to about 8 million by September.

![Bar chart of Saudi oil: East-West pipeline flows rising from 2 to 7 million barrels per day, and output of 10 million before the war versus 8 million in September](/images/2026-10-23-iran-warns-us-bases-as-hormuz-tanker-attacks-hit-october-high/saudi-oil.svg)

### A second front near Bab el-Mandeb

The Houthis and Saudi Arabia have escalated fighting in Yemen, which AP says threatens the Bab el-Mandeb shipping corridor. Al Jazeera reported that an attack on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam on Wednesday killed two people and wounded eight, and Fox News reported that the US ordered non-emergency government staff and families to leave Saudi Arabia. Earlier attacks are covered in our [report on Houthi strikes on Saudi airports](/blog/2026-10-19-houthi-attacks-on-saudi-airports-draw-egypt-pledge/). The strait's role in global food supply was examined in [our look at the stalled Hormuz fertiliser plan](/blog/2026-10-15-hormuz-fertiliser-plan-stalls-un-warns-of-food-crisis/).

## What to watch

- **US response:** whether Washington continues or expands escorts after the IRGC warning. The U.S. is also sending a third aircraft carrier and thousands of troops to the region, AP reported.
- **Possible new strikes:** Fox News, citing a New York Times report, said the US and Israel are drafting plans to <mark>resume strikes after 3 November</mark>, targeting energy, nuclear, and missile and drone sites. This is reported planning, not a decision.
- **Election calendar:** Israel votes on 27 October and the US midterms are on 3 November, both of which AP flagged as context.
- **Shipping data:** whether Lloyd's List and UKMTO counts keep rising, and whether insurers or shippers change routes.
- **Diplomacy:** any sign that indirect talks revive Iran's proposal to reopen the strait in exchange for ending the blockade.

## Editor's Insight

The notable shift is in the logic of the standoff. For months the escorts and the blockade were competing pressure tools: the US squeezes Iran's exports, and Iran squeezes everyone else's shipping. The IRGC's statement widens the stakes by naming US bases, not just ships, as potential targets. That is a warning rather than an action, and a threat is cheaper than a strike, so it may be aimed as much at bargaining as at battle.

The economic picture is more fragile than the headline flow numbers suggest. If flows are near pre-war levels, as AP reports, the cost has moved into insurance, freight rates and risk: tanker rates in the hundreds of thousands of dollars a day, ships going dark, and shippers reluctant to report incidents. Saudi Arabia's growing pipeline route reduces its exposure but shifts risk to the Red Sea side, where the Houthi-Saudi fighting is now escalating. These are possible readings of reported facts, not forecasts.

Politics is a visible overlay. Trump has tied Iran's actions to the midterms, while the New York Times report cited by Fox News places planning for new strikes in the period after 3 November. Whether any side chooses de-escalation before then will depend on decisions not yet reported, and readers should treat claims from the parties, including those on oil volumes, as unverified until confirmed by independent data.
