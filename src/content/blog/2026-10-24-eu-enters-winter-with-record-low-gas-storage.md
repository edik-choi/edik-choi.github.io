Europe is heading into winter with <mark>gas storage about 72% full, against a usual 90%</mark>, Fatih Birol, head of the International Energy Agency (IEA), told Al Jazeera on 23 October. He said the Iran war has cut Gulf supplies and left the continent exposed to higher prices if the cold is severe. The warning raises the question of how Europe will keep heating homes and running industry through the coming months.

![Key-figure card: EU gas storage about 72% full versus a usual 90%, a shortfall equal to the energy for 10 to 12 million homes](/images/2026-10-24-eu-enters-winter-with-record-low-gas-storage/key-card.svg)

## What happened

### How low is EU gas storage this winter?

In the interview, conducted in Berlin, Birol called Europe's gas stocks "much lower than historical averages." According to Al Jazeera, the EU enters winter with storage about 72% full, compared with a usual level of around 90%. Analysts quoted by the outlet say the shortfall is <mark>equal to the energy needed to power 10 to 12 million homes</mark>. Al Jazeera's headline describes the reserves as the lowest on record.

"Life will not be easy for Europe this year and also next year," Birol said. He added that "if we have a harsh winter, we may see higher prices of natural gas in Europe." Al Jazeera reports that <mark>gas prices have risen 140% since February</mark> and that Brent crude is trading well above $100 a barrel.

### What are governments considering?

EU energy ministers are reportedly considering asking households and businesses to ration consumption, according to Al Jazeera. Birol said governments should have taken the necessary measures earlier, and that energy "is not currently considered a business but a political leverage." He hopes the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab al-Mandeb strait will be "unconditionally opened sometime soon," and said Middle East countries are building pipelines to bypass Hormuz.

## Background

### Why did the Iran war hit Europe's gas supply?

Before the war, nearly a tenth of Europe's liquefied natural gas (LNG) passed through the Strait of Hormuz, according to Al Jazeera, and Iranian attacks on vessels have sharply reduced those imports. Europe also competes for LNG with Asian buyers, which depend more heavily on the Middle East. Flare-ups in the Bab al-Mandeb strait have made matters worse. Our earlier report on [Iranian warnings and rising tanker attacks near Hormuz](/blog/2026-10-23-iran-warns-us-bases-as-hormuz-tanker-attacks-hit-october-high/) describes the shipping risk that sits behind these shortages.

### Why was storage already so low?

Analysis from Columbia University's Center on Global Energy Policy, published on 23 April, found that <mark>Europe began the 2026 refill season with 31 billion cubic metres (bcm) in storage</mark>, the lowest level since 2018. Total EU capacity is about 110 bcm. The EU requires storage to reach 90% by 1 November, a target that was not met in 2025, and the author said Europe must refill without most Russian pipeline gas and with an indefinite loss of Qatari LNG. Germany and the Netherlands were flagged as the main risk areas, while Italy and France had managed storage better through stricter national policies.

### How did oil and diesel prices add to the squeeze?

Gas is only one part of the picture. Euronews reported on 29 September that the IEA had released 400 million barrels of oil from strategic reserves since March, with roughly a third not yet having reached the market, and that about 80% of stocks remained in reserve. EU Energy Commissioner Dan Jørgensen said the bloc had spent <mark>more than €100 billion extra</mark> on energy imports since the war began, which he said brought "not one extra molecule of gas or oil."

![Bar chart of EU diesel prices per litre: EU average 2.23 euros, France 2.40, Denmark 2.56](/images/2026-10-24-eu-enters-winter-with-record-low-gas-storage/diesel-price.svg)

The same report said the EU's average diesel price reached a <mark>record €2.23 per litre</mark>, with €2.40 in France and €2.56 in Denmark, and that the US supplied about half of EU diesel imports in August. Birol told Al Jazeera that Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries have <mark>cut about a third of Russian deliveries</mark>, contributing to a looming diesel shortage. We covered that campaign in [Ukraine's decision to resume refinery strikes](/blog/2026-10-16-ukraine-ends-energy-truce-and-resumes-refinery-strikes/).

## Why it matters

### Who is exposed if the winter is cold?

The EU's 27 member states are home to hundreds of millions of people, and a cold snap would hit heating bills and industrial output across its member states at the same time. Birol linked a harsh winter directly to higher gas prices. Because Europe bids against Asia for LNG cargoes, tight European demand can also push up prices for buyers elsewhere, which is why the story reaches beyond the continent.

![Diagram of pressure points on Europe's winter energy supply: the Strait of Hormuz, Asian competition for LNG, Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries and the Bab al-Mandeb strait](/images/2026-10-24-eu-enters-winter-with-record-low-gas-storage/pressure-points.svg)

### What does it mean for policy?

Brussels plans to phase out Russian gas permanently, yet Al Jazeera reports that France and others have recently increased orders amid the Gulf crisis. That tension shows how quickly security goals can collide with the need to keep supply flowing. Birol's remark that "the best day for a ceasefire would have been yesterday" underlines that the IEA sees diplomacy, not stock releases, as the main fix.

## What to watch

- **Storage trend:** whether EU storage holds near 72% or falls faster than usual as heating demand rises from November.
- **Rationing decision:** whether EU ministers formally ask households and businesses to cut use.
- **Hormuz and Bab al-Mandeb:** any change in attacks on shipping, since Birol ties relief to both straits reopening.
- **Further stock releases:** the IEA has said more releases are possible if members agree, though it has not made them a top priority.
- **Diesel supply:** Russian refinery output and US export policy, which Oxford Economics said could move European diesel prices sharply if US exports were restricted.

## Editor's Insight

The central lesson of this story is that Europe's energy security now depends on a chain of chokepoints it does not control. Gas storage is the visible buffer, but the figures in this post show how many links feed it: Gulf LNG, Asian demand, Russian refinery output and American diesel. A shock to any one link lands on the same already-thin reserve.

There is also a policy trade-off. The EU has spent over €100 billion more on imports without gaining extra supply, according to Jørgensen, which suggests money alone cannot buy security in a physically constrained market. If Brussels moves to ration demand, it would shift the debate from price to fairness, since households and industry feel cuts differently. That is a possibility, not a forecast the sources make.

Finally, Birol's comments hint at a longer horizon. He expects difficulty "this year and also next year," and Columbia's analysis doubted that Qatari output would return to full capacity by early 2027. If those assessments hold, this winter may be the first of several tight ones, which could accelerate investment in bypass pipelines, storage and demand reduction. Whether governments act on that is an open question.
